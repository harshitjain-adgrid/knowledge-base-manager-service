# Who Gets Your Offer, and How Often

Every offer carries two controls that are easy to mix up: **who** is allowed to
use it, and **how many times** it can be used.

## Choosing the audience

An offer goes to one of four groups, or to a single customer:

| Audience | Who it means |
|---|---|
| All customers | Everyone |
| New customers | People who have not bought from you before |
| Loyal customers | Your regulars |
| Lapsed customers | People who used to come and have stopped |

Targeting is a real filter, not a label. A customer outside the group simply
does not see the offer when they scan your QR.

## The two different limits

These two settings sound alike and do completely different jobs:

- **Uses per customer** — how many times **one person** may use this offer. Left
  unset, one person can use it as often as they like.
- **Total customers** — how many **different people** may use it at all. Left
  unset, there is no cap.

They multiply. Five customers allowed, five uses each, is up to twenty-five
redemptions in total.

## Picking sensible limits

- **A welcome offer** for new customers usually wants uses-per-customer set to
  one — the point is the first visit.
- **A quiet-hours discount** usually wants no per-customer limit, because you
  want the same people coming back at that time.
- **A loss-leading deal** wants a total-customers cap, so the cost is bounded
  however popular it turns out to be.

## Only one offer applies to a payment

If a customer qualifies for more than one of your offers, they get **the one
that saves them the most**. Offers do not stack, and the customer is not asked
to choose the worse one.

This matters when you run a second offer: a new small discount may never be seen
if an existing bigger one keeps winning.

## What if nobody is redeeming your offer

- **The audience may be too narrow.** An offer aimed only at lapsed customers
  reaches nobody if most of your customers are regulars.
- **The minimum bill may be too high.** If it sits above what people normally
  spend with you, almost no payment qualifies.
- **The limits may already be used up.** An offer that has hit its total limit
  stops appearing even while it looks live.

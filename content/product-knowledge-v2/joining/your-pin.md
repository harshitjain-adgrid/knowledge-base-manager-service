# Your PIN and What It Authorises

You set one PIN, once, shortly after your first login — and that same PIN is
reused every time you do something that moves money or changes your account.
Merchants call it *PIN* or sometimes *password*. It is the difference between
someone holding your unlocked phone and someone able to act as you.

## The steps to reset a forgotten PIN

1. Choose the option to reset your PIN when you are asked for it.
2. LessPay sends a one-time password to your registered mobile number.
3. Enter that one-time password.
4. Choose your new PIN.
5. Watch for the confirmation message that your PIN has changed.

## One PIN, reused everywhere

There is not a separate PIN per feature. The same one authorises:

- Taking and confirming payments
- Settlements to your bank
- Sensitive changes to your account

That is deliberate — one secret to remember, asked for at each point that
matters.

## Which actions ask for your PIN

| Action | Needs the PIN |
|---|---|
| Unlocking the app | Yes |
| Creating a discount or a deal | Yes |
| Deleting an offer | Yes |
| Adding, switching or deleting a QR code | Yes |
| Changing your shop profile | Yes |
| Looking at payments, settlements, wallet, offers | No |
| Asking Chotu a question | No |

The rule behind the list: **reading is free, changing money-related things is
not.** There is no "stay unlocked for ten minutes" and no way to authorise
several actions with one entry — every sensitive action asks again, on its own.
That is inconvenient by design, so a phone left on the counter cannot be used to
create offers or move your QR codes around.

## Your PIN never leaves your phone as you typed it

The PIN is scrambled on your own device before it is sent, using a key that
belongs only to that device. Nobody at LessPay can read it, and it is not stored
anywhere in a form that could be read back.

This is why support cannot tell you your existing PIN when you forget it. There
is nothing to look up, so resetting it is the only route.

## Every change is announced

Setting or changing your PIN always sends you a confirmation, and that message
includes a way to act if it was not you.

**Treat an unexpected PIN-change message as a warning, not noise.** It is the
one notification you should never scroll past, because it is what tells you
somebody else is in your account.

## Confirming is not optional

When Chotu proposes something that touches money — creating an offer, changing
where your settlements go — it stops and asks you to confirm with your PIN. It
does not act first and tell you afterwards.

That is the boundary: Chotu can prepare anything, and you authorise it.

## What if you have forgotten it

Reset it. The one-time password goes to your registered mobile number, which is
why that number needs to be one you still have. If it is not, the reset cannot
reach you and support has to be involved.

## Hinglish mein

PIN ek hi hota hai aur wahi baar baar kaam aata hai — payment lene mein,
settlement mein, aur account ke zaroori badlav mein. Agar PIN bhool jaye to
reset kar sakte hain: aapke registered number par OTP aata hai, wo daal kar naya
PIN bana lijiye. Jab bhi PIN set ya change hota hai, ek confirmation message
aata hai. Agar wo message aaye aur aapne kuch na kiya ho, to samajhiye koi aur
aapke account mein hai — turant PIN badliye.
